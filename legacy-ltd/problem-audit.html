Stage One of Eight

Before you answer anything, understand what you're wagering.

Most business surveys are useless because everyone answers them the way they'd like to be seen. This one is built so that isn't worth doing. Four things you should know before you start.

Lying costs you money

This ends in a spend recommendation. Overstate a problem and you'll be told to spend real money fixing something that isn't broken. Understate one and your money stays where it is, leaking.

"I don't know" is a scored answer

Several questions ask for numbers. Not knowing is a legitimate, expected response and it is scored as information — it tells us something true. Guessing is the only answer that corrupts the result.

You'll be caught contradicting yourself

Answers are cross-checked against each other. Where two of your responses can't both be true, you'll be shown the pair and asked which one stands. That's a feature.

Confidence is weighted, not assumed

Every number you give is tagged with how you know it. Measured figures count in full. Gut feel counts for less. So inflating a number reduces its influence rather than increasing it.

Stage Four of Eight

If you could buy improvement in only these five areas, and you had exactly one hundred chips to spend, where would they go? You can't give everything a ten here — every chip you put in one place is a chip you took from another. That trade-off is the point.

Stage Five of Eight

Sort them. Only six can be bleeding.

These are drawn from 126 evergreen problems, weighted toward where you spent your chips. Every one has to go somewhere. Bleeding is capped at six — if everything is urgent then nothing is, and a cap forces you to reveal what you'd actually protect first.

Stage Six of Eight

How often does it bite, and what does it cost when it does?

No dollar figures — you'd be guessing, and a guess dressed as a number is worse than no number. Answer only from what you've lived: how often this actually costs you something, and what it costs in the units you already think in. We'll do the arithmetic, and show you every step of it.

Stage Seven of Eight

Where your answers disagree.

Nothing here is a trick. These are places where what you told us in one stage can't comfortably sit beside what you told us in another. Pick which version stands — the ledger is built from your choice.
